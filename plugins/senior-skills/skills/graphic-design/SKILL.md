# Graphic Design

Graphic design is **visual communication** — using layout, type, color, and imagery to
carry a message to an audience and make them feel and do something. Good design is not
decoration; it is the message made clear, memorable, and appropriate. Start from the
goal and audience, build on the fundamentals, and finish with production files that
actually reproduce correctly.

## Core loop

1. **Brief** — the message, audience, goal, medium, constraints, and brand.
2. **Gather & concept** — content, references, and a few distinct creative directions.
3. **Compose** — structure the layout with hierarchy, grid, space, and alignment.
4. **Craft** — typography, color, and imagery working as a system.
5. **Critique & refine** — against the goal; squint, get distance, cut.
6. **Produce** — correct formats, color space, bleed, and resolution for the medium.

## 1. Brief (design is problem-solving)
- Pin the **message** (the one thing it must say), the **audience** (who, what moves them),
  and the **goal/action** (inform, persuade, sell, delight). Everything serves these.
- Capture the **medium and constraints**: print or screen, exact size/format, where it lives
  (billboard vs. phone vs. business card — viewing distance changes everything), budget (number
  of print colors?), copy, required elements (logo, legal), and deadline.
- Capture the **brand**: existing identity, voice, and assets to stay consistent with
  (`references/branding-and-identity.md`). Ambiguity here wastes the whole effort.

## 2. Gather & concept
- Collect the real content (copy, images, logo, data) — design *with* actual content, not lorem
  that hides length problems. Gather references/mood for tone.
- Generate a few **distinct** directions (thumbnails/sketches) before committing — explore wide,
  then narrow. Avoid polishing the first idea; the first idea is rarely the best.

## 3. Compose (structure first)
- Establish **visual hierarchy**: what the eye sees 1st, 2nd, 3rd — via size, weight, color,
  contrast, and position. One clear focal point. If everything shouts, nothing is heard.
- Use a **grid** for alignment and consistency; align everything to something. Use **white
  space** deliberately — it's not empty, it gives breathing room and focus.
- Balance the composition (symmetry vs. intentional asymmetry), guide the eye along a path, and
  apply **Gestalt** grouping (proximity, similarity, alignment) so related things read as related.
  See `references/composition-and-layout.md`.

## 4. Craft (type, color, image as a system)
- **Typography** (`references/typography.md`): choose typefaces that fit the tone; limit to
  1–2 families with clear roles; set a type scale for hierarchy; mind spacing (kerning/leading/
  line length) and, above all, **readability**. Type is most of graphic design.
- **Color** (`references/color.md`): a disciplined palette (often one dominant + support +
  one accent) built on harmony; use it for hierarchy and meaning; ensure **contrast** (and
  accessibility where it carries info); set up the right color space for the medium.
- **Imagery:** photos/illustration/icons of consistent style and sufficient quality; respect
  licensing; crop and treat them to support the message, not fight the type.

## 5. Critique & refine
- Judge against the **goal**: does a first-time viewer get the message and know what to do, at the
  real viewing distance and size? See `ui-ux-design`'s critique reference for structured critique.
- Techniques: squint (does hierarchy survive?), view at final size/distance, flip horizontally or
  step away (spot imbalance), go grayscale (does it work without color?), sleep on it.
- **Refine by subtraction** — remove anything not serving the message; tighten alignment and
  spacing; check consistency. Proofread the copy (typos kill credibility). Get a second eye.

## 6. Produce (reproduction is part of design)
- Set up for the medium (`references/production.md`): **print** = CMYK (or spot), 300 dpi,
  bleed + safe margins, vector logos, correct trim size, packaged fonts/links, PDF/X. **Screen**
  = RGB, correct pixel dimensions (and @2x/@3x), optimized PNG/JPG/SVG/WebP, sRGB.
- Keep an editable master + organized, well-named exports. A beautiful design that prints muddy,
  pixelates, or has elements cut off at the trim is a failed design.

## Operating principles
- **Communication first.** Design serves the message, audience, and goal — not the designer's taste.
- **Hierarchy is everything.** Guide the eye deliberately; one clear focal point.
- **Less, but better.** Remove the unnecessary; white space and restraint read as quality.
- **Type carries the message.** Readable, well-set, appropriate type is most of the job.
- **Consistency = system.** Repeat type, color, spacing, and style; align to a grid; honor the brand.
- **Design with real content**, at real size, for the real medium and distance.
- **Produce correctly.** Right color space, resolution, bleed, and format — or it won't reproduce.
- **Respect licensing** of fonts, images, and assets.
