# Typography

Type is most of graphic design — it carries the message and sets the tone. Good
typography is first **legible and readable**, then expressive. Craft lives in the details.

## Choosing typefaces
- Classify and choose for **tone**: **serif** (traditional, authoritative, editorial),
  **sans-serif** (modern, clean, neutral), **slab serif** (bold, sturdy), **script** (elegant/
  casual — sparing use), **display** (distinctive — headlines only, never body). Match the
  typeface's personality to the message and audience.
- Favor families with **range** (multiple weights/styles) so you can build hierarchy within one
  typeface. Quality fonts are worth it; respect **licensing** (desktop vs. web vs. embedding).

## Pairing (keep it simple)
- **1–2 typefaces** with clear roles (e.g. a characterful display/heading + a highly readable
  body). Three is a lot; more usually looks chaotic.
- Pair by **contrast, not conflict**: a serif with a sans is a reliable combo; pairing two similar
  sans fonts often looks like a mistake. A single well-chosen family across weights is often best.
- Ensure a clear role difference (don't pair two display faces fighting for attention).

## Hierarchy & scale
- Build a **type scale** (e.g. a modular ratio) so sizes relate harmoniously; use size + weight +
  case + color + space to separate levels (display → heading → subhead → body → caption).
- Make steps **clearly distinct** — timid 1–2px differences read as errors, not hierarchy.

## Spacing & readability (the craft)
- **Line length (measure):** ~45–75 characters per line for body text; too long tires the eye,
  too short fragments reading.
- **Leading (line height):** body usually ~1.4–1.6× size; tighter for large display, looser for
  long text. Too tight or too loose both hurt.
- **Kerning/tracking:** fix awkward letter gaps; tighten large headlines slightly; add a little
  tracking to all-caps and small text. Don't track body text loose.
- **Alignment:** left-aligned (ragged right) is most readable for long text. Justified needs good
  hyphenation to avoid rivers. Centered only for short bits (titles); never long paragraphs.
- **Paragraphs:** indicate with space or indent, not both. Avoid widows/orphans (a lone word/line
  stranded). Keep a comfortable size — err larger for body.

## Expression & polish
- Use **case** deliberately (ALL CAPS for short emphasis, not sentences). Emphasize with weight or
  italic, not underline (underline reads as a link). Use real small caps, proper quotes/apostrophes
  (" ' not " '), en/em dashes, and ligatures.
- Limit effects — avoid stretching/condensing type artificially, heavy drop shadows/outlines, and
  rainbow coloring. Let a good typeface do the work.

## By medium
- Print can carry finer type and detail (high dpi). Screen needs sufficient size and contrast;
  very thin weights and tiny text fail on screen and for low-vision readers. Consider contrast
  (see `color.md`) — type that carries meaning must meet contrast needs.

## Common pitfalls
- Too many typefaces; pairing near-identical fonts; display faces as body text.
- Weak hierarchy (sizes too close); lines too long; leading too tight.
- Justified text with rivers; centered paragraphs; all-caps for long copy.
- Fake bold/italic or stretched type; straight quotes and hyphens used as dashes.
- Light-gray thin type with poor contrast; ignoring font licensing.
