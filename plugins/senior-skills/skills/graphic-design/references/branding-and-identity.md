# Branding & Identity

A brand is how an organization is perceived; the **visual identity** is the system of
elements that makes it recognizable and consistent. Design identities as reusable
*systems*, not one-off pictures.

## Logo design
- A logo is an identifier, not the whole brand. Aim for:
  - **Simple** — recognizable and reproducible at a glance (the best logos are deceptively plain).
  - **Memorable & distinctive** — stands apart from competitors; not a cliché of the category.
  - **Versatile** — works across sizes (favicon → billboard) and contexts (on light/dark, color/
    mono, embroidered, embossed). Test tiny and huge.
  - **Appropriate** — fits the organization's sector and personality.
  - **Timeless** over trendy — it must last years, not chase a fad.
- **Always design in vector** (scales infinitely). Provide variants: primary, secondary/
  horizontal/stacked, icon/mark only, and **one-color/black/white/reversed** versions.
- Types: wordmark (logotype), lettermark (initials), brandmark/symbol, combination mark, emblem.
  Choose by name length, recognition needs, and flexibility.

## Identity system (beyond the logo)
A coherent identity defines and consistently applies:
- **Logo** + its variants and **clear space / minimum size** rules.
- **Color palette** — primary/secondary/accent with exact values in Hex/RGB/CMYK/Pantone (`color.md`).
- **Typography** — brand typefaces and how they're used (headings/body/accent), with a fallback
  for web/system where licensing requires (`typography.md`).
- **Imagery style** — photography/illustration/iconography direction (treatment, mood, do's/don'ts).
- **Graphic elements** — patterns, shapes, grids, motifs, and layout conventions that recur.
- **Voice & tone** — how the brand sounds (pair with `technical-writing`) — identity is verbal too.

## Brand guidelines
- Package the system into **brand guidelines** so anyone can apply it consistently: the logo and
  its correct/incorrect usage (what *not* to do — don't stretch, recolor, add effects, crowd),
  color and type specs, imagery rules, spacing, and examples across real applications
  (business card, social, signage, packaging, web).
- Consistency *is* brand strength — the same elements repeated everywhere build recognition. The
  guidelines protect that at scale.

## Process
- Start from brand strategy: who they are, their values, audience, competitors, and desired
  perception. Design the identity to express that, not the designer's taste.
- Explore multiple directions; test the logo in real contexts and at real sizes early (not just
  on a pristine presentation board). Design the *system*, then show it applied.

## Common pitfalls
- Overcomplicated logos that die at small sizes or in one color; trendy marks that date fast.
- Delivering only a full-color raster logo (no vector, no mono/reversed variants).
- A logo with no surrounding system (color/type/imagery), so applications look inconsistent.
- No usage rules → the brand gets stretched, recolored, and distorted in the wild.
- Designing the identity around aesthetics instead of the brand's strategy and audience.
