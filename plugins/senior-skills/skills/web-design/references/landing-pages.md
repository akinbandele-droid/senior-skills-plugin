# Landing Pages & Conversion

A landing page has one job: move a specific visitor to take one specific action.
Every element either advances that or dilutes it. Design for the decision, not
just the look.

## Start from the goal
- **One primary conversion goal** per page (sign up, buy, book, download). Name it.
- Know the **audience and traffic source** — an ad click, a search result, and a
  referral arrive with different context and intent; the message must match the
  ad/promise that sent them (message match).
- Define the single primary **call to action**; everything supports it.

## Structure that converts
1. **Hero (above the fold):** a clear value proposition — what it is, who it's for,
   why it's better — in a headline a visitor gets in ~5 seconds, plus a prominent
   primary CTA. Avoid clever-but-vague headlines.
2. **Value / benefits:** lead with outcomes for the user, not feature lists. Show,
   don't just tell (screens, demo, before/after).
3. **Social proof:** testimonials, logos, numbers, reviews, case studies — specific
   and credible beats generic praise.
4. **Objection handling:** address the top reasons people hesitate (price, risk,
   effort, trust) — FAQ, guarantee, security/privacy cues.
5. **Repeat CTA:** restate the action at natural decision points and at the end.
6. **Minimal footer:** keep the exit ramps few on a focused landing page.

## Calls to action
- Make the primary CTA visually dominant (contrast, size, whitespace) and
  unmistakable. One primary per section; secondary actions clearly subordinate.
- Action- and value-oriented copy: "Start my free trial" > "Submit".
- Reduce friction: fewest form fields possible, no surprise steps, clear what
  happens next. Reassure near the button (no card required, cancel anytime).

## Focus & friction
- Remove distractions that don't serve the goal — extra nav, competing links, and
  unrelated content leak conversions. (Dedicated campaign pages often drop the main nav.)
- One idea per section; strong hierarchy; generous whitespace; fast scan.
- Clarity beats cleverness. Confused visitors leave.

## Trust & credibility
- Real images/specifics over stock vagueness; show the product.
- Visible trust signals: reviews, guarantees, security/compliance, real company info.
- Honest claims — overpromising kills trust and retention.

## Performance & technical (conversion killers)
- **Speed is conversion:** a slow hero/LCP loses visitors before they read. Optimize
  the above-the-fold path hard (see `web-performance.md`).
- Mobile-first: most traffic is mobile; the CTA and value prop must shine on a phone.
- Accessible: keyboard, contrast, labels — excluded users don't convert.

## Measure & iterate
- Instrument the funnel; know where people drop.
- A/B test one variable at a time (headline, CTA, hero) against a real metric;
  decide by data, not taste. Avoid testing noise on low traffic.
- Iterate on the biggest drop-off first.

## Quick checklist
- Value proposition clear in 5 seconds? One obvious primary CTA above the fold?
- Benefits (not just features)? Credible social proof? Top objections answered?
- Distractions removed? Form as short as possible? Fast on mobile? Accessible?
- Message matches the source that drove the traffic?
